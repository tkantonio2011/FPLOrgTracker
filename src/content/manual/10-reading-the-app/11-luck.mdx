The Luck Ranking tries to answer the most contentious question in FPL: am I good or am I lucky? It does this by building a per-manager expected-points baseline (xPts) from the underlying FPL data — minutes, expected goals/assists, bonus probability — and comparing your actual points against that expectation.

<AnnotatedImage src="luck/overview.png" caption="The Luck Ranking page comparing actual vs. expected points per manager." />

## What the ranking shows

Each manager has three numbers and a delta:

- **Actual points** — your real season total.
- **Expected points (xPts)** — what your team should have scored based on the underlying chances created, minutes played, and shot quality.
- **Δ** — actual minus expected. Positive means you got more than your team's underlying performance suggested (you've been lucky); negative means you got less (you've been unlucky).
- **Luck rank** — 1 = luckiest, last = unluckiest. Independent of the Standings rank.

The page sorts by Δ descending — luckiest manager at the top, unluckiest at the bottom.

## How xPts is calculated

For each player you own, in each gameweek:

- Expected minutes from FPL's projected lineup data
- Expected goals + assists from underlying shot quality (xG/xA)
- Expected appearance, clean sheet, and goal-conceded contributions
- Expected captain multiplier applied to your actual captain

These get summed across your starting eleven (or 15 for Bench Boost weeks). Bonus is approximated from BPS expectation rather than awarded explicitly.

This isn't a true model of FPL — it's a heuristic. But over a full season it's a much better signal of "how well did you pick" than total points alone, because points are noisy and xPts smooths the noise out.

<Callout type="note" title="A high actual rank with low luck rank is a real flex">
  If you're in the top 3 by points AND ranked 8th by luck (meaning you've been more unlucky than most), you're playing genuinely well. If you're top 3 by points and 1st by luck (meaning the dice landed in your favour all season), enjoy it while it lasts — that's regression to the mean waiting to happen.
</Callout>

## What the model can't see

- **Differential picks** that work — if you correctly identified a low-owned player who outperformed his xPts, you'll appear lucky even though it was insight. Pure-stats models can't tell genius from variance.
- **Chip timing** — playing your Bench Boost on the right double-gameweek is a skill the model rates as "lucky" because all 15 of your starters scored.
- **Knowing when to take a hit** — a successful -4 for an injury replacement looks like luck to the model.

So treat the Luck rank as a conversation starter, not a verdict.
