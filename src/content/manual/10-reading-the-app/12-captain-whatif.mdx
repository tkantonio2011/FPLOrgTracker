Captain What-If quantifies the cost of every captain pick. For each gameweek, it compares what your actual captain scored (after multiplier) against what your *best owned alternative* would have scored. The gap is the points you left on the table.

<AnnotatedImage src="captain-whatif/overview.png" caption="The Captain What-If bar chart with colour-coded misses per gameweek." />

## How to use it

<Steps>
  <Step title="Pick a manager">
    Defaults to you. The dropdown lets you analyse any league member.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read the season summary tiles">
    Optimal picks count, total misses, biggest single-GW miss, and average miss per GW.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Scan the bar chart">
    Green = you picked the optimal captain. Amber = small miss (≤4 pts). Orange = moderate (≤10 pts). Red = catastrophic (>10 pts).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Drill in to a specific GW">
    Below the chart, the per-GW breakdown table shows your captain (with points), the best alternative you owned (with points), and the score impact.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What "best alternative" means

The page considers only players that were in your squad that gameweek — including bench players. The "best alternative" is whoever scored highest. So if you captained Salah for 8 and a bench midfielder you owned scored 18, the miss is `(18 × 2) − (8 × 2) = 20 points`. Ouch.

This is the realistic ceiling — captaincy is a choice between players you actually owned. The page doesn't compare against players you didn't own (that would be the global ceiling, a different and arguably more painful number).

## Triple Captain weeks

If you played Triple Captain that week, the multiplier is 3 instead of 2 for both the actual and the hypothetical. The page reflects this in the GW row.

## Why this matters

Captain points are roughly **20% of your weekly score** on average. Optimising captaincy is the single highest-leverage decision you make each week. A manager who consistently picks the optimal captain (green bars all season) is doing one thing extraordinarily well, even if everything else is mediocre.

<Callout type="warning" title="Don't rage-quit on hindsight">
  The page shows the optimal pick *after the fact*. The point is to identify patterns over time — e.g. you systematically captain the wrong forward when there's a rotation risk. Picking on a single bad GW is not a strategy.
</Callout>
